Management of Acute Diarrhoea and Dehydration in Children

Synthetic test text for software testing only. Not clinical guidance.

Acute diarrhoea is one of the leading causes of illness in children under five, especially where safe water and sanitation are limited.

Assessment of dehydration. Look at the general condition of the child, check whether the eyes are sunken, offer fluid to see whether the child drinks eagerly or is unable to drink, and pinch the skin of the abdomen. Classify the child as having no dehydration, some dehydration or severe dehydration.

Treatment plans. Children with no dehydration can be treated at home with extra fluids and continued feeding. Children with some dehydration receive oral rehydration solution (ORS) under observation in the clinic. Children with severe dehydration need urgent intravenous fluids; if IV access is not possible, a nasogastric tube can be used for ORS while arranging referral.

Zinc supplementation is given alongside ORS. Breastfeeding and feeding should continue during the episode.

Settings with limited supplies. Where intravenous fluids or equipment are limited, prioritise ORS, train caregivers to give it at home, and refer children with severe dehydration or danger signs as soon as possible.
